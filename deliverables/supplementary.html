2. Target Group & Sampling Method目标人群与抽样方法

2.1 Target group目标人群

Respondents are Year 1 students at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University who are currently enrolled on an EAP module. The sample spans all four EAP levels — A (foundation stream), B and C (core streams, where most students are placed) and High Level (advanced stream) — and covers arts, science and engineering programmes so that the picture reflects the whole Year 1 cohort rather than a single school.

2.2 Sampling method抽样方法

We used convenience sampling, a non-probability method: the questionnaire link was distributed through EAP class group chats and year-group chats. This was chosen because the Year 1 cohort is large and no complete sampling frame was available to us. The recognised cost is that cell sizes are uneven across levels, which is why non-parametric checks are reported alongside the parametric tests and the limitation is stated openly in the conclusions.

3. Study Design调研设计

This is a cross-sectional survey with a non-experimental design: usage and attitude data are collected at a single time point, and no variable is manipulated. We measure differences and associations as they occur naturally, so every finding is correlational rather than causal.

3.3 Why these evaluation methods为什么选这些评估方法

Each test was matched to the shape of the data on three criteria: (i) the measurement level of the variables — chi-square for categorical pairs, ANOVA or correlation for scale variables; (ii) the design — a one-way ANOVA for four independent conditions, a paired test for any pre/post comparison; and (iii) whether the parametric assumptions hold, checked with Shapiro–Wilk for normality and Levene for homogeneity of variance. Where an assumption failed, the corresponding non-parametric test was used instead. Because cell sizes are uneven across the four levels, effect sizes (Cramér's V, η²) are reported alongside p values. All tests are two-tailed at α = 0.05.

Structured online questionnaire, approximately 6–8 minutes to complete. Closed items precede the single open item.

  2. Have you used Smart Partner?Yes / No — if "No", skip to Question 8
  3. How many times a week do you use it on average?Numeric entry
  4. How long is a typical session?<10 / 10–20 / 20–30 / 30–45 / >45 minutes
  5. Which features do you use most?Multi-select: grammar practice / writing feedback / vocabulary consolidation / mock EAP tests / academic English Q&A / exam revision planning

  7. How confident are you about passing the EAP exam?1 (not at all confident) – 10 (extremely confident)
  8. What is the single biggest improvement Smart Partner needs?Open-ended, optional

5. Instrument — Interview Guide调研工具 · 访谈提纲

Semi-structured interview, approximately 15 minutes, conducted with participants who opted in. Questions are prompts rather than a fixed script; interviewers follow up on whatever the participant raises.

  1. In what context did you first come across Smart Partner?
  2. Describe one time it was especially helpful — or especially useless.
  3. Would you recommend it to a classmate? Why or why not?
  4. Has it changed how you prepare for EAP? In what way, specifically?
  5. If one feature could be added, what would you want it to be?

9. Reflection研究反思

9.1 Methodological limitations方法局限

The sample is a convenience sample and is concentrated in the two core streams, B and C, which is where most students actually sit. The foundation and advanced streams therefore contribute far fewer respondents, and the resulting unbalanced cell sizes affect the robustness of the ANOVA — which is why non-parametric tests were also run on the ordinal items. The questionnaire is self-report, so frequency and duration are subject to recall bias; future work should cross-validate against server-side usage logs and interview High Level students to find out why they use the agent least.

9.2 What went wrong in practice执行过程中遇到的问题

  1. Uneven level sizes — convenience sampling left groups A and High Level thin, so the planned equal-cell comparison was impossible; we switched to reporting effect sizes and adding non-parametric tests.
  2. Ambiguous wording — in the pilot, several students read "use frequency" as app opens rather than complete sessions; the item was redefined as "sessions" with a clarifying note.
  3. Self-report bias — some students read "the week before the exam" as "the whole exam month", inflating duration estimates; trends are therefore reported rather than exact values.
  4. No causal claim — a cross-sectional design cannot tell whether using the agent raised confidence or more confident students were keener to use it, so the correlation is flagged explicitly as non-causal.

9.3 What we would change next time改进方向

  1. Switch to stratified sampling with quotas across the four EAP levels, at least 50 respondents per cell.
  2. Wire in server-side usage logs to cross-validate self-reported frequency and duration.
  3. Add a longitudinal design: measure confidence at the start and end of term, replacing cross-sectional correlation with a paired test.
  4. Run targeted interviews with High Level students to explain their lower adoption.

10. Research Ethics研究伦理

All participants saw the information sheet and ticked the consent form before answering. Data were anonymised, containing no names, student IDs or other identifiers, and participants could withdraw at any time without affecting their grades. Results are reported in aggregate only and are not traceable to any individual. Interview recordings, where made, were used solely for transcription and were deleted afterwards.
